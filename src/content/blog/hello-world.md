"Hello, world" is a phrase known to almost every programmer in the world.
A program that prints out this phrase is usually the first program that one
would create when learning to program or try out a new programming language. In
fact, it is that common that this phenomenon has its own
[Wikipedia page][hello-world-wiki]. These programs don't serve that much
practical use, but are usually used to demonstrate syntax and as a sanity check
to verify proper installation. Despite this, I still vividly remember the first
time I wrote such a program and the sheer thrill that I had made a computer do
such a thing. Up to this very day, it still always feels like a little bit of magic
to me.

Outside of programming, the phrase is well known among golfers after one of my
heroes, golfer Tiger Woods, used it to introduce himself upon his professional
debut. Although Tiger did not play that great in this specific tournament
(although he did make a hole-in-one), his success in the early stages of his
career has made this moment an iconic piece of golfing history.

My fascination with Tiger has never really been about the superhuman results.
Comparing myself to that would be ridiculous, and golf already has enough
delusional wannabe pros convinced they're the next one. What actually draws
me in is that he didn't just win inside the existing category of "great
golfer," he redefined what that category could even look like, rewriting the
approach to the game itself. That's the part I relate to. I've never quite
fit inside one box either: not fully a data person, not fully an engineer, not
fully a business person, but some category of my own stitched together from all three.

Writing is the thread that ties those boxes together, and it's the reason I'm
starting this blog. I've always been driven by an unrelenting curiosity to learn as much as I possibly could. The compounding effect of this has definitely led to a lot of questions along the lines of "how do you know these things?" or "you should really start a podcast, I could listen to this for hours". The world doesn't exactly have a shortage of podcast bros right now, so don't worry, that's not on the cards. What I actually want is to create an outlet for structuring and connecting these learnings; a place to make the dots I connect in my head
legible to someone else. Having great ideas and vast knowledge isn't all that
useful if you cannot communicate them adequately. Or as Nelson Mandela once
put it a little bit more eloquently: "A good head and good heart are always a
formidable combination. But when you add to that a literate tongue or pen,
then you have something very special".

Blogging also lets me share my thoughts with the world and give people a way
to get familiar with some of my past and current projects. Not only do I
thoroughly enjoy learning about new things, sharing this newly gained
knowledge is almost equally rewarding, and it doubles as a portfolio.

So, what to expect here. This blog runs on two tracks: essays like this one,
and a more technical track covering products, technical breakdowns and demos.
Some weeks that means a reflection, other weeks a build log. Either way, I'll
try to make it worth your time.

Cheers,
Felix
